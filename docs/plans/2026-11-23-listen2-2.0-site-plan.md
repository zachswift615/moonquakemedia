- **Accent slots.** Each roster voice in `src/_data/listen2Voices.json` has an `accent` field, `null` until Zach's

## 3. Voice samples: slots and file naming

**One slot per voice**, in `src/_data/listen2Voices.json` under `roster`. Today it lists the 24 voices built on
2026-11-23 (roster analysis), in alphabetical order, with `"ready": false` and `"nameFinal": false`. When Zach's
picks are in (release plan D7, E3, E4), replace the list with the final voices in the order the app shows them.

**Convention** (based on the roster ids, which are also the voice ids in `listen2-engine-manifest.json`):

| What | Path | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One sample per voice | `src/assets/listen2/voices/<voice-id>.m4a` | e.g. `voices/callum.m4a`. Lower-case id exactly as in the manifest. |
| Hear-it passage, pace demo | `src/assets/listen2/voices/hear-it/pace-<step>--<voice-id>.m4a` | `<step>` is `slowest`, `normal` or `fastest`. |
| Hear-it passage, voice row | `src/assets/listen2/voices/hear-it/voice--<voice-id>.m4a` | Three Listen2 Voices. |
| Hear-it passage, other engines (off by default) | `src/assets/listen2/voices/hear-it/engine--<engine>-<voice-id>.m4a` | `<engine>` is `phonon`, `supertonic` or `apple`. Only if Zach says yes (question 4). |
| Highlight demo video (optional, like the current Phonon ones) | `src/assets/listen2/web/demos/listen2-<voice-id>-<book-slug>.mp4` plus `.jpg` poster and `.vtt` | Same convention as the existing `phonon-hadley-pride-and-prejudice.*`. |

Why a new `voices/` folder and not the existing `samples/` folder: `samples/` holds the Voice Library samples for
every engine, including names that must not appear in marketing, and it already has a file called
`listen2-en_US-zara.mp3`, which is not a Listen2 engine voice. A `listen2-<id>` name in that folder would be
confused with it.

**Audio spec (proposal):** AAC in `.m4a`, mono, 64 to 96 kbps, rendered at the model's 24 kHz. Every voice at
its default Voice Profile, the way the app first plays it (the new voices' default Pace and Liveliness, unless a
voice's own profile overrides it; Pace Normal and Liveliness Most lively, question 17). Loudness matched across
voices, about 8 to 12 seconds. The same passage for every voice so people compare voices, not texts. Render once
and publish what comes out: if a take misreads, fix the cause or drop the voice from the grid; do not re-render
until a lucky take appears. The page copy says the samples are as the app makes them, so they must be.

**Sample text (proposal; question 10):** reuse whatever passage the app's own sample button plays (track G2), so
the site and the picker sound the same. If the app's passage is not settled, this one has a date, a number and a
heteronym without being a test:
"Chapter one. On the morning of the 3rd of May, I read the letter twice before I opened the window. It said the
house had been empty for forty-two years."

**How a slot renders:** with `ready: false`, a dashed box with the voice's name, a NAME NOT FINAL tag when
`nameFinal` is false, the label SAMPLE SLOT and the expected path. With `ready: true`, a native
`<audio controls preload="none">` with the name as its label. The page builds either way; the audio files are
only referenced when `ready` is true.

**Screenshot slots** (`listen2Voices.json` → `screens`): `web/voice-profile.webp`, `web/create-voice-card.webp`,
`web/create-voice-consent.webp`, `web/create-voice-result.webp`, and a new `web/voice-library.webp` (the current
one shows only Phonon and Supertonic). See section 8 for what each must show and must not show.

## 4. The "Hear it" section

**Problem first:** a reading voice is easy to judge on a line of poetry. Real documents are full of the hard
parts: a time, a price, an initialism, a word whose sound depends on its meaning. So the section shows one
paragraph that has all of them, as text on the page, and lets people hear it.

**The passage** (in the data file, so it can be changed in one place):

> Last Tuesday I read the 42-page PDF on the 7:45 train. Today I will read it again, because the lead engineer
> found lead in the 1920s pipes, and the repair will cost $18,400. Her résumé lists six years at NASA and two at
> the FDA.

It contains: "read" said two ways, "lead" said two ways, an accented heteronym (résumé), a time, a decade, a
price, a hyphenated number, an initialism spelled out (PDF, FDA) and one said as a word (NASA). The M1 must listen
to every render of it before it goes up, because this section stands or falls on the audio being right.

**Layout:**
- Left (or top on phones): the passage in a reader card (Surface `#131315`, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 21/1.5),
  with one word carrying the dark-mode highlight as a still image of the signature. No fake moving highlight:
  without real timings it would not follow the audio, and the brand rule is that the highlight follows speech.
  The real moving highlight is on the demo videos above.
- Right (or below): two groups of sample slots.
  1. **One voice, three paces.** Slowest, Normal, Fastest, same voice. This is the most defensible thing on the
     page: the fact sheet measured seven steps, each faster than the last, with the same voice. The middle slot
     is Normal, the default step (label confirmed); "Voice decides", where the model picks its own pace, is not
     demoed.
  2. **Three voices, one paragraph.** Three Listen2 Voices of different character (Zach picks).
- A third group, **The same paragraph from each engine in Listen2** (Listen2, Phonon, Supertonic, Apple), is
  built but switched off (`hearIt.showOtherEngines: false`). Recommendation: leave it off. The fact sheet says
  we have tested no other product and must not imply a comparison; putting our engine beside Apple's voice
  invites exactly that reading. Question 4.
- Under it, a `<details>` "How we measured this" with the method and its limits, in plain words (6,027 clips,
  about 9 hours, two speech recognizers, no listener panel, the recognizers' own mistakes, "modern words" is our
  own list).

No JavaScript: every slot is its own native player, so it works with VoiceOver and keyboard as is, and a
VoiceOver user hears a labelled "Pace Slowest, Callum" player rather than an unlabelled toggle.

## 6. Unsupported claims (wanted to say, could not)

Stated plainly. None of these is on the page.

1. **Anything about speed, battery, heat, app size, download size or which phones run the Listen2 Voices.** The
   phone measurements do not exist (FS "Not measured yet"; release plan J2). So the FAQ's device answer has a
   CONFIRM gap where the Listen2 Voices should be.
2. **"The Listen2 Voices come with the app" or "work the moment you install it".** Release plan D1 bundles the
   Core ML package and has older phones download the 8-bit package, and the device line (D2) is not measured. I
   cannot say who gets them without a download. The hero no longer says "come with the app" about any voice set
   except Phonon.
3. **"Clone your voice", "sounds like you", "sounds exactly like your dad".** Similarity is 0.41 on unseen voices
   against 0.82 for two recordings of the same person (FS). The honest promise is "shaped by your recording".
   This also means the "my dad reads my books now" video idea (CMO handoff, item 11) must not suggest the voice
   is his.
4. **Accents of the Listen2 Voices, by guesswork.** Zach, 2026-11-23: label each voice American or British, or
   another accent such as Scottish where confirmed. He is labelling them by ear, so the grid has an empty `accent`
   slot per voice and no label is shown until his list arrives. On the site, Phonon groups British and Scottish
   voices as Great Britain (question 12).
5. **Other languages for the Listen2 Voices.** English only.
6. **What Liveliness does** ("more expressive", "emotional", "storytelling"). It has not been evaluated. The page
   names the control and its steps and says nothing about its effect.
7. **"Never misreads", "100% accurate", "never skips", "never repeats".** The measured figures are about 99 in
   100 words and zero skips in this test set. Zero in a test is not "never".
8. **"The rest are mostly the checker's mistakes."** This is in the fact sheet's own draft line 3, but the
   measurement row only supports "the true rate is a little better". The page uses the weaker wording.
9. **"Most reading voices stumble on modern words."** Also a fact-sheet draft line (line 1), but it is a claim
   about other products, and the fact sheet says we have tested none. Not used.
10. **Better than, or indistinguishable from, a human reader**, or "rated by listeners". The naturalness score is a
    machine's estimate. No listening study exists.
11. **Any comparison with another product**, named or implied, including Apple's voices and other reading apps. Hence the other-engines row in "Hear it" is off by default.
12. **How accurate "words by meaning" is.** No figure for the shipped heteronym model is in the fact sheet (the
    97.5% in `docs/espeak-removal/g2p-models.md` is for an earlier model and is not marketing-cleared). The page
    describes the feature and makes no accuracy claim.
13. **How strong the consent check is** ("cannot be fooled", "secure", "only you can make your voice"). The 0.45
    line gives about 0.1% false accepts and 2% of true speakers rejected, measured in simulation; the file path
    against a phone-microphone sentence was only simulated (design §9, decision 1: the studio-versus-iPhone check
    is still to do). The page repeats the design's own caveat: it cannot catch every imitation.
14. **That the Bluetooth and car fixes work.** They are compiled, the tests were not run, and nothing was tried on
    a device or in the car. The card is not rendered (`showBluetoothCard: false`) until Zach's QA lines pass.
15. **What the 13-language pronunciation models improve, and which voices they serve.** The voices they serve are
    ones the site must not name, and no quality comparison with the old component is in a marketing-cleared
    document.
16. **What "retrained voices" means for the listener.** Which voices were retrained and how they sound different
    is not in any source I was given. Not on the page.
17. **Playback speed up to 2.5x for Listen2 Voices.** The profile design says speeds above 2.0 do nothing extra on
    Listen2 Voices (clamped). The existing side list says "Speed from 0.5x to 2.5x", which stays true for other
    engines; it is left as is but should not be repeated next to Listen2 Voices.
